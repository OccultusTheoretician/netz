KNP-26 §5.02 · trust nothing, recompute

Verify a Kall

Paste a reveal block. Your browser reconstructs the canonical preimage per the construction published in the hashlog itself (§4.01b), recomputes SHA-256 locally via WebCrypto, and compares it to the sealed commitment on the public log. Nothing is sent anywhere; the desk is not consulted; the desk could not interfere if it wanted to.

The reveal block

A reveal is JSON published by the committer when a Kall is opened — at minimum the fields named by the construction (for this log: id, timestamp, statement, resolution_basis, salt).

What a match proves — and what it does not

A match proves this exact content existed, unedited, at the sealed timestamp — before the outcome. It does not prove the call was wise, that resolution was fair, or anything about the sealed-but-unrevealed remainder. Per KNM 6.03 those discretions survive the mechanism and are disclosed, not hidden. The count of what remains dark is public: that is the whole design.
