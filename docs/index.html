A method for telling foresight from arithmetic — and a desk that runs it in the open, against its own record, misses printed.

The method

A Retro-Prescient Audit examines a prescience claim the way an auditor examines a charter: it sets the claim against the record and scores the distance. It runs both directions in time. Retro — take a foresight record someone claims and test it against the archive, misses counted. Prescient — seal new predictions under commitment before the outcome, so the forward record accrues where no one, the forecaster included, can quietly edit it.

  1. Pre-registration. A prediction counts only if written, with its failure condition, before the outcome. A prediction produced after the reveal is not a prediction.
  2. The keyed/keyless split. A hit deducible from priors already in hand is inference, not foresight, and is claimed only as inference. The audit’s first job is to take that cloak.
  3. Misses print. Permanently. A record that lists only its hits is promotion; a run with no recorded misses is presumed corrupt.
  4. Named baselines. A hit counts only above what chance or base rates would produce from the same inputs. The feeling of having called it is not a result.
  5. Blind adjudication. The adjudicator does not see the forecaster’s identity or stated probability, must cite evidence, and may return ambiguous.

The full definition is sealed in commit history: RETRO_PRESCIENT_AUDIT.md.

Standing

As of this reading

This is an instrument being tested in public, not a validated one. Under thirty resolved predictions the score is noise — and the ledger says so on its own face. The record currently shows the machine missing more than it hits.

That number is the credential. A desk that prints its own bad news is the only kind whose good news means anything. Consistency is not correctness — resolution is.
