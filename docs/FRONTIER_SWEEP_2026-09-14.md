## 1. Audit-trailed, hash-frozen LLM forecasting evaluation with disclosed corrections
- Occupied, and close kin: arXiv 2608.20304 (Aug 2026), "Calibration-Induced Degeneracy in LLM Financial Forecasting: An Audit-Trailed Case Study on Next-Day Market Risk" - a prespecified, hash-frozen design (dates, endpoints, fitting rule, retries, bootstrap, code hashes, spending cap fixed before any outcome was opened), a coverage gate that failed on the first pass, and a procedural correction disclosed "as a substantive procedural correction, not invisible preprocessing"; the paper itself notes its hashes "establish file identity, not an independent timestamp", so the design is "internally prespecified and hash-frozen rather than preregistered". That last sentence is the exact boundary the desk crosses with OpenTimestamps and a host whose clock it does not control.
- What survives as the desk's own: none of the discipline is novel; the desk's mark is that the registration, the estimator's hash, the checks and the read are on a public, externally anchored record, and that the null (Finding 11) was printed under the registration's own preconditions. Cite as the nearest occupant of "audit-trailed LLM forecasting evaluation".

## 2. Temporal leakage and the same-day window

## 3. LLM-as-judge reliability and the cold seat's kappa
- Occupied: arXiv 2606.19544 (Jun 2026), "Reliability without Validity: A Systematic, Large-Scale Evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge Models Across Agreement, Consistency, and Bias" - 21 models, three benchmarks, 541,000 judgments; the finding that raw agreement overstates discriminative ability and chance-corrected measures (Cohen, Krippendorff) are required. arXiv 2512.01786 ("Who Judges the Judge? - Consequence for the desk: the blind jury prints kappa on every sitting (+0.018 on 2026-09-08, the cold seat abstaining by rule on 98 of 101), which is the chance-corrected figure this literature asks for; the clerk pass (load-bearing citations verified at primaries before adoption) is the human-validation loop the guides describe. The structural open stands as recorded: the protocol cannot detect confabulation that lands on the correct verdict. Nothing here is the desk's; the desk's mark is the printed kappa and the printed abstention rule.

## 4. Known-answer vectors for an evaluation standard (DRIFT-26 Chapter 13)
- Occupied, three ways: (a) the Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative (IBSI) - digital phantoms with benchmark values so that independent radiomics implementations agree on computed features, the mature form of the idea; (b) arXiv 2607.00297 (EPC, 2026) - "a reference test suite ... verifies that independent implementations produce identical gamma and JSD values on fixed input sequences ... passing the conformance suite qualifies an implementation as EPC-v1.0-compatible", a conformance suite for a statistical protocol, published weeks before DRIFT-26 rev0; (c) US patent 12645555, "Systems and methods for deterministic conformance suites, validator implementation certification, and verifiable certificate registries for replay-verifiable conformance receipts" - deterministic test vectors with expected validator outputs (PASS/FAIL/HOLD), a conformance certificate committing to suite digest and validator digest. The patent occupies the certification-registry layer, not the vector idea itself, which is decades old (NIST CAVP known-answer tests).
- Consequence: DRIFT-26 13.02's sentence that reproducing the vectors lets an implementer compute the standard "without trusting the reference build or its author" overstates (the assistant's rework note of 2026-09-07 stands); the vectors pin the reference's behaviour. Chapter 13 should cite IBSI and EPC as the form, and the standard should say what the 09-07 note says: the one non-circular result is 9.01a. The patent is cited as a boundary the desk does not enter: the desk issues no certificates and keeps no registry.
