## NOTE 1 - The same-packet, frozen-rubric version pair (H4)

**Construct.** Two versions of one model family (here `manual/fable-5/unattested`
and `manual/fable-5.1/unattested`) price the same elicitation packet on the same
day under the same rubric, whose SHA-256 every row carries (`rubric_hash`), for
the life of one cohort. Version drift is then the per-decile difference in
observed frequency between the two arms within that cohort, read by the
pre-registered test of the Kalibrierwarte registration, Section 6, hypothesis
H4: for each decile in which both arms clear the bin floor, the observed
frequencies differ by more than the wider of the two bootstrap 95 percent
intervals. Floors, checkpoints and the six quality checks are stated in the
registration before any arm reaches its first checkpoint; the estimator
(`warte_report.py`) is pinned by hash in Section 13 and the sections defining
the hypotheses freeze for an arm at its first checkpoint.

**Record.** The pair has accrued daily since 2026-09-03 (fable-5 rows since
2026-08-14 under the earlier arm tag): as of this note 231 rows under fable-5 and
96 under fable-5.1 in the running cohort, none resolved within it. The read is
UNTESTABLE by the registration's own floors and prints as such. The first
checkpoint of the design's local sibling (`lmstudio/auto`, cohort 4ea5ab8f,
n = 96) produced the registration's first confirmatory read on 2026-09-09: H2
NOT SUPPORTED, skill -0.094 [-0.448, 0.084] against the base-rate control
(Finding 11).

*Correction, 2026-09-14 (RPAS-26 5.07), printed by the desk's assistant, whose error it
is.* The Record above states 231 fable-5 rows "in the running cohort" and dates the
lineage to 2026-08-14. Neither is what the ledger this note was cut from

106 rows under `manual/fable-5/unattested` and 96 under `manual/fable-5.1/unattested`,
none resolved; 231 is the first arm's total across every cohort (113 under 4ea5ab8f,

**Occupied.** Rolling leaderboards compare model versions across time
(ForecastBench, arXiv 2409.19839; Prophet Arena; Metaculus FutureEval; FutureX;
HINDCAST, arXiv 2607.14051). Pre-registration and hash-frozen designs for LLM
evaluation exist (arXiv 2608.20304, a hash-frozen financial-forecasting case

**Held.** A pre-registered, same-packet, frozen-rubric paired design across two

## NOTE 2 - The keyed/keyless determination

**Construct.** Before a forecast resolves, and never after, the desk records
whether a hit would be deducible from the priors the forecaster declared - the
numbered items of the record it cited (RPAS-26 4.02f). KEYED: the cited record
supplies the claim's subject and reaches toward the outcome; a hit is
arithmetic on the record. KEYLESS: no cited item is sufficient; a hit would be
a claim beyond the record. A row that resolves without a determination is KEYED
by rule (4.03) and can never bear on a faculty claim. Only keyless rows bear on
one (5.04). The doctrine errs toward the smaller keyless count by construction:
the machine may concede a row keyed on printed evidence (`--keys-propose`); only
the operator may claim one keyless, with the rule cited; corrections before
resolution are dated entries carrying the superseded value (5.07).

**Record.** 862 determinations on the ledger before this note; 939 written in one
pass on 2026-09-09 and 23 on 2026-09-14 (all keyed but six). 109 keyless
determinations in all; 35 keyless rows resolved, 10 hit. The verifier prints the
count of rows resolved without a pre-resolution determination (23, all from
sittings before the cadence law) and it has not moved since.

**Occupied.** Contamination and temporal leakage in LLM forecasting evaluation
(Paleka et al., arXiv 2506.00723; arXiv 2602.00758 on date-filtered retrieval
leakage) name the adjacent hazard: the outcome reaching the forecaster. Forecast
"resolution" criteria pre-stated before outcomes are standard practice in
forecasting tournaments (GJP, Metaculus).

**Held.** The determination itself - deducibility from declared, enumerated
priors, ruled before resolution, printed per row with its rationale, corrected
only by dated superseding entry - as the gate on any claim of skill beyond the
record. Not located as a named construct in three sweeps.

**Verify.** `python rpas_verify.py` recomputes the seal of every row (the
determination is one of the eight sealed fields) and prints the 4.03 count;
`ledger.json` carries `keyed_keyless`, `keyed_keyless_rationale` and
`keyed_keyless_dated` per row.

## NOTE 3 - The committed count as a completeness control (DECC-26)

**Construct.** When a record-keeper discloses part of a machine-generated
population, DECC-26 requires the population's cardinality to be committed
(hashed, anchored) with the disclosure and printed with any verdict drawn from
the disclosed part, together with the surviving discretions on the verdict's
face (Chapter 8). The reference implementation (`denom.py` 0.1.0) carries 38
known-answer vectors and eight forgery classes a conformant build must refuse.

**Record.** In force since 2026-07-30; the desk's own conformance printed at
Level 3 (`decc_conformance.py`); the PCAOB 2026-005 comment docket held as a
committed population of 33 letters (2026-08-18); the Finding 9 correction and
the Revision 2 text (9.04) printed the prior art below upon discovery.

**Occupied.** Completeness proofs over committed populations are established in
authenticated data structures for outsourced databases (Devanbu et al. 2002;
Pang and Tan 2004; Pang et al. 2005; Mykletun, Narasimha and Tsudik 2006; Li et
al. 2006) - stronger constructions than this control, which binds only the
count. Certificate Transparency (RFC 6962) binds a log's size into its signed
tree head. A design-science proposal of 2026-07-09 (Wright, "The Audit Evidence
Problem Public Ledgers Were Supposed to Solve") reaches the same architecture
for selective disclosure of accounting fields three weeks before DECC-26's
first issuance and states population completeness as unsolved by it. The
priority is the proposal's.

**Held.** Only 9.02 as re-read in 9.04: the committed cardinality as a
completeness control for partial disclosure of audit evidence, printed with the
verdict, under a conformance suite that can fail an implementation. Not located
in that specific form. Stated conservatively (9.03).

**Verify.** `python denom.py verify-bundle bundle.json --anchor anchor.json`
against any served bundle; `python decc_conformance.py` on a clone.

## NOTE 4 - Calibration drift under a frozen rubric as a measured quantity (DRIFT-26)

**Construct.** Drift is defined not as a change in a leaderboard score but as a
difference in observed frequency per stated-probability decile between two
arms that priced identical elicitations under one hashed rubric, read with a
pre-registered test, floors and checks, by an estimator whose behaviour is
pinned by known-answer vectors (DRIFT-26 draft rev0, Chapter 13: seven vectors,
7/7 against the pinned estimator). The one non-circular result of the standard
is its check (e) clarification: a constant-priced control has skill exactly zero
only when its rate equals the realized rate of the rows it mirrors; the check
tests the reference class, can fail at large n on a small gap, and can pass on
interval width at small n while the gap stands (9.01a).

**Record.** Check (e) failed on 2026-09-03 at control n = 12 (Finding 10) and
passed on width on 2026-09-09 at n = 22, whereupon the first confirmatory read
ran (Finding 11). Both are printed; neither repaired.

**Occupied.** The form - known-answer values and phantoms so independent
implementations agree - is mature (IBSI digital phantoms and benchmark values
for radiomics features; EPC, arXiv 2607.00297, a conformance suite verifying
identical statistics on fixed inputs across implementations; NIST CAVP
known-answer tests). A US patent (12645555) occupies deterministic conformance
suites with certificate registries; the desk issues no certificates and keeps
no registry. Calibration of LLM confidence is a large literature; calibration
scored against prediction markets exists (Kalshibench, arXiv 2512.16030).

**Held.** The quantity - per-decile frequency difference under a frozen, hashed
rubric on identical elicitations - with its floors, checks, estimator pin and
vectors stated before the data, as a standard a stranger can apply to any two
arms. Not located in that form. DRIFT-26 is a draft and says so.

**Verify.** `python drift_vectors.py` reproduces the seven vectors against the
pinned estimator (LF-SHA-256 fa560fe3...); the registration and the reports are
in `docs/` and `forecasts/`.
