## Definition

A Retro-Prescient Audit is a scored, misfire-inclusive examination of prescience claims —
the discipline of testing whether a stated foresight record is real foresight or
retrospective arithmetic dressed as foresight. It runs both directions in time:
**retro**, auditing claims already made against what the record shows happened after;
**prescient**, sealing new predictions under commitment before outcomes, so the forward
record accrues where it cannot be edited.

## The laws

1. **Pre-registration.** A prediction counts only if written, with its failure condition,
   before the outcome — sealed by hash commitment or an equivalent third-party timestamp.
   A prediction produced after the reveal is not a prediction.
2. **The keyed/keyless split.** A *keyed* hit is one deducible from priors already
   available to the predictor — inference with a fixed key: arithmetic, not foresight.
   A *keyless* hit had no prior sufficient to deduce it. Only keyless hits count toward
   a foresight claim; keyed hits are scored, labeled, and claimed only as inference.
3. **Misses print.** The deliverable is a score that includes the misses, permanently.
   A record that lists only hits is promotion, not evidence. A run with zero recorded
   misses is presumed corrupt.
4. **Named baselines.** A hit counts only above what chance, base rates, or generic
   cold-reading would produce from the same inputs. Every audit names its baseline
   before scoring.
5. **Blind adjudication.** Where practicable, resolution is performed by an adjudicator
   who does not see the forecaster's identity or stated probability, must cite evidence
   for each verdict, and may return AMBIGUOUS.
6. **Disconfirmation binds.** A claim the audit cannot in principle score negative is
   outside the method's jurisdiction and is labeled as such.

## Operating instance

The KKR predictive ledger in this repository is a standing Retro-Prescient Audit run
against a machine forecasting system: hash-sealed issuance, dated deadlines, blind
adjudication packets, Brier scoring with calibration bins, and misses and voids kept
on the permanent public record.

## Scope

The method audits records and claims, not persons; its output is a score, never a
certification. Consistency is not correctness — resolution is.
