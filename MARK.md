# THE MARK — Nebelkrähe

*Specification of record. Revision 1, 2026-07-27.*

One geometry, one generator, six files. Everything the site serves is built from
`mark_build.py`; nothing downstream is hand-edited. This is the same rule that
governs `ledger.json` and its served copy, applied to the crest — because the mark
had already fragmented into four incompatible copies before anyone noticed.

---

## 1. WHAT IT IS

A crow in flight, beak forward, tail dissolving into fog. Thirty vertices, all
straight edges, no curves anywhere. Two grey panels on the body, a grey trim
running the neck, and a slit eye.

The bird is not decoration. On the front page it rides the radar sweep at the
leading edge of the beam, one rotation every twenty-two seconds, beak pointing
along the direction of travel. The beak is the bearing indicator.

---

## 2. PROVENANCE

The artwork is the author's own crest, drawn as a flat polygonal silhouette. The
vector table was recovered from the raster original by boundary tracing:

- Source: `Occultus_PROFILE_nebelKraehe.png`, 1024 × 1024, and an earlier
  745 × 340 render.
- The two agree to a scale of 1.003 and a rotation of 0.19° — identity within
  tolerance — verified by matching the eye centre and the beak tip in both.
- Outer contour traced by Moore boundary following, then simplified by
  Ramer–Douglas–Peucker at a 2.5px tolerance: 1,955 boundary pixels collapse to
  **30 vertices**, which is the proof the original was drawn as straight facets.
- Grey panels segmented by luminance: the ink sits flat at L=31, the panels at
  L=183, the background at L=215. Belly 11 vertices, shoulder 5.

Two elements in the raster original are **not** part of the mark and were
removed: the perch (a bright full-width band starting at y≈295) and two twig
fragments, dropped as separate connected components.

## 3. GEOMETRY

All coordinates in the source frame, before the 4px pad.

| element | vertices | note |
|---|---|---|
| body outline | 30 | closed, self-touching at the neck apex |
| shoulder panel | 5 | clipped to the body |
| belly panel | 11 | clipped to the body |
| neck trim | 8-point polyline | stroked, clipped to the body |
| eye slit | 4 | drawn last, never clipped |

**The neck apex, (266, 123).** The contour touches itself here: the head's
trailing edge arrives, and the body's leading edge departs from the same point.
It is one vertex, not a junction, and the head's back edge runs to it unbroken
from (256, 27). The slit below it opens at 105°.

**The eye.** A four-point sliver, 34 long by 5 thick, canted 13°, asymmetric —
sharp forward toward the beak, fuller aft. It reads as a narrowed glare rather
than a bead. The coordinates are pinned verbatim in the generator rather than
recomputed from angle and length, because rounding order lands them half a pixel
away and the approved artwork is the artwork.

**The trim.** A 22px stroke centred on the neck contour and clipped to the body,
so exactly 11px shows inside. Miter joins, butt caps: round joins put blobs on
the feather spikes, which is wrong on a design with no curves in it.

**The panels.** Widened 15% across their short axis only, then translated along
that axis toward the body's centre by half the gain — so the outer edge stays put
and the growth goes inward. Scaling about the centroid instead pushes half the
gain outside the silhouette, where the clip eats it, and the panel comes out
*thinner*. Length is untouched; the mark does not stretch toward the tail.

## 4. COLOUR

| token | value | use |
|---|---|---|
| ink | `#0f1216` | head, body, wing |
| grey | `#b3b9b3` | panels and neck trim, taken from the original at L=183 |
| light | `#eef1f5` | the eye slit only |
| plate | `#0e0f11` | site background, for opaque icon plates |

The tail is a single `linearGradient` from the beak tip to the tail tip:

```
0.00  #0f1216  1.0     ink
0.60  #0f1216  1.0     ink
0.73  #343b42  1.0     grey begins, through the wing serration
0.84  #464e56  0.9
0.93  #535b63  0.5
1.00  #5d656d  0.0     fog
```

The wing tips sit at 0.76–0.83 along that axis, so they take the grey from the
same ramp as the tail. There is no separate wing colour.

## 6. USE

- Minimum width 44px. Below that the serration and the slit stop resolving; use
  the square lockup instead.
- Never recolour, never outline, never add a drop shadow inside the artwork. The
  page applies `filter: drop-shadow()` around it, which is a page property, not a
  mark property.
- Never stretch. The aspect is 729:300 and the square lockup exists so nobody has
  to squash it into a box.
- The mirrored form (`scaleX(-1)`) is correct and expected wherever the bird must
  fly clockwise — the radar sweep uses it so the beak leads the beam.
- Do not edit the SVG by hand. Edit `mark_build.py` and regenerate; anything else
  reintroduces the drift this document exists to end.

---

## 7. HOW IT IS WIRED

`mark_apply.py` points every surface at the generated files and is safe to re-run:

- `docs/*.html` — masthead image, icon links, `og:image`, twitter card type
- `netz.py` — the `CROW_SVG` constant, previously a ~40KB base64 PNG baked into
  every generated page, now a file reference; and the generated pages' own icon
  link

Generated faces (`kkr.html`, `ledger.html`, `report.html`) take the mark from
`netz.py` and pick it up on the next `python kkr.py --score`.
